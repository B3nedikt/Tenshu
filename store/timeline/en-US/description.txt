Tenshu Timeline records the places you visit and the trips between them, on your phone. Your timeline is encrypted before it leaves your device, and only your own devices can read it.

TENSHU TIMELINE NEEDS TENSHU CONTROL
Tenshu Control holds your keys and connects your devices. Install Tenshu Control from the App Store and set it up first, then open Timeline. Timeline finds Control on its own.

WHAT YOU CAN DO
- Turn on Record places and trips in Settings. Timeline asks for Always location access, so it can record while it is closed. iOS wakes Timeline when you arrive somewhere or leave, so a very short trip can be missed.
- Read your days as a feed: the places you stayed and the trips between them, with each trip's route, distance, duration and how you traveled.
- When a run of days looks like a journey, Timeline suggests grouping it. Accept it or say it was not a trip.
- See all your places and their visits. Name a place, merge two that are the same, or choose the right place for a visit.
- Use Apple Maps for the maps, if you prefer. It is off until you turn it on.

HOW TIMELINE KEEPS YOUR LOCATION PRIVATE
- Timeline records your location only after you turn on Record places and trips.
- Your places and trips are encrypted on your device before they are uploaded. Tenshu's servers store only encrypted data, and they don't have the keys to read it.
- The server can still see some facts it needs to sync: how much you store, how many devices you have, and when they sync.
- On iPhone and iPad, Timeline does not look up place names, so it does not send your coordinates to a place-name service. You name your places yourself.
- If you turn on Use Apple Maps, Apple serves the map, and sees which place or trip you open and when.
- Without a storage plan, your timeline stays on this device.

REQUIREMENTS
- iOS 16 or later, on iPhone or iPad.
- Tenshu Control, set up on the same device.
- To sync between devices: a storage plan. The free plan has 5 GB.
