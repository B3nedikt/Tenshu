Tenshu Contacts keeps your contacts encrypted and in sync across your devices. Only your own devices can read them.

TENSHU CONTACTS NEEDS TENSHU CONTROL
Tenshu Control holds your keys and connects your devices. Install Tenshu Control from Google Play and set it up first, then open Contacts. Contacts finds Control on its own.

WHAT YOU CAN DO
- Add contacts, and edit their phone numbers, email addresses, organization and notes.
- Bring people in from this phone: pick one contact, import a .vcf file, or import your whole address book.
- Find a contact by name, phone number, email address or organization.
- Invite someone with a one-time QR code or link. When they accept, compare a six-digit code to confirm it is them.
- Verify a person you connected with, and see a warning when their key changes.
- Find possible duplicates, choose the one to keep, and merge them.
- Set the name the other Tenshu apps show for you.

HOW CONTACTS KEEPS YOUR CONTACTS PRIVATE
- Your contacts are encrypted on your device before they are uploaded. Tenshu's servers store only encrypted data, and they don't have the keys to read it.
- The server can still see some facts it needs to sync: how much you store, how many devices you have, and when they sync.
- Tenshu has no directory, so nobody can look you up. You connect with someone only when you both accept an invitation.
- Contacts reads your address book only when you choose to import from it, and it never writes to it.
- Without a storage plan, your contacts stay on this device.

REQUIREMENTS
- Android 7.0 or later.
- Tenshu Control, set up on the same phone.
- To sync between devices: a storage plan. The free plan has 5 GB.

Tenshu is open source and still in testing: expect rough edges, and tell us what breaks.
