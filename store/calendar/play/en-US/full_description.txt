Tenshu Calendar keeps your events and reminders encrypted and in sync across your devices. Only your own devices can read them.

TENSHU CALENDAR NEEDS TENSHU CONTROL
Tenshu Control holds your keys and connects your devices. Install Tenshu Control from Google Play and set it up first, then open Calendar. Calendar finds Control on its own.

WHAT YOU CAN DO
- See your month, week or day, or a list of what is coming up.
- Add, change and delete events: all-day or timed, with a location, notes and a repeat. Change one occurrence of a repeating event, or all of them.
- Get reminders before an event, even when Calendar is closed, and snooze or dismiss them from the notification. Hide an event's title on the lock screen for a calendar you choose.
- Keep several calendars, each with its own color. Show or hide each one on this phone.
- Subscribe to a calendar by its web address, such as public holidays or a team schedule. It updates on its own, and you can pause it.
- Import an .ics file into a calendar, or export a calendar as an .ics file. Open .ics files from other apps in Calendar.
- See what is next on your home screen with the widget.

HOW CALENDAR KEEPS YOUR EVENTS PRIVATE
- Your events and calendars are encrypted on your device before they are uploaded. Tenshu's servers store only encrypted data, and they don't have the keys to read it.
- The server can still see some facts it needs to sync: how much you store, how many devices you have, and when they sync.
- When you subscribe to a calendar, your device downloads it directly from its publisher, and the publisher sees your IP address. Each of your devices downloads it for itself.
- Calendar does not read the other calendars on your device.
- Without a storage plan, your events stay on this device.

REQUIREMENTS
- Android 7.0 or later.
- Tenshu Control, set up on the same phone.
- To sync between devices: a storage plan. The free plan has 5 GB.

Tenshu is open source and still in testing: expect rough edges, and tell us what breaks.
